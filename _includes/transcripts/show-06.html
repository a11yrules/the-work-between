I'm disabled, and I'm very conscious of how much some of this advice assumes about the person holding the sketchbook. Urban sketching can make demands on your body before you've even started drawing. I often have to use wheelchair if I see a building I want to draw. I can't necessarily wander around until I find exactly the composition I want, and then settle wherever happens to give me the best view. Filtering all of that can take effort, and that leaves less attention available for figuring out the drawing. For me, something different happens. If I'm sitting somewhere in public with a sketchbook, I'm very aware of the fact that I'm drawing in public, I'm trying to figure out a roof line. Someone walks toward me. I can't help wondering: Are they going to stop? Are they going to look? Can they see the page? And suddenly, I'm aware of the drawing in a completely different way. I'm aware of my hand. I'm aware that the line isn't right. I'm aware that somebody might see that it isn't right. Maybe they don't care. They probably don't care. I'm still learning perspective, and an experienced sketcher may look at a building and understand that those lines are doing almost without thinking about it, I don't always see it yet. At home, I can be a learner. I can spend 20 minutes getting one roof line wrong. Maybe I start again and get it wrong a second time. Maybe I make a complete mess of the page.
