This episode is a little different. Instead of interviewing someone else about their creative life, I invited my friend and colleague Mark Miller to interview me about mine.

We talk about the three creative practices I keep coming back to: sketching and watercolor, quilting, and wildlife photography. They’re very different activities, but they all do something similar for me. They quiet my brain and give me a break from accessibility work, screens, and the constant need to advocate as a disabled person.

Mark asks about where these interests came from, why I’m drawn to urban sketching and photographing birds, and why I care more about the process of making something than the finished result. We also talk about the influence of judo and tai chi, the value of learning through repetition, and the satisfaction of catching the decisive moment in photography.

We get into some less comfortable territory too. I talk about being my own worst critic, wishing I had stuck with art when I was younger, and the secret sketchbook I use for work I have no intention of showing anyone.

Mostly, though, this is a conversation about being more than one thing. I’m an accessibility specialist and a disabled person, but I’m also a husband, a son, a photographer, a quilter, a sketcher, and someone who will probably keep finding new things to make.

### A quote that stayed with me

> "Maybe the practice I'm using right now is different than the one I'm using six months down the road, but I'm still practicing creativity rather than not sticking to one thing."

### About Mark

Mark Miller works in digital accessibility and inclusion. He is also a photographer, studied studio art, practices martial arts, and co-hosts the Inclusion Impact podcast. For this episode, I gave him carte blanche to turn the tables and interview me.
